The Angkor Sacred Code of Conduct

Angkor is not a cinematic stage; it is a living sanctuary of active Theravada worship and centuries-old stone reverence. Strict enforcement is upheld by heritage police.

checkroom
RULE 01 DRESS CODE

Cover Shoulders & Knees

No bare shoulders, sheer fabrics, crop tops, or shorts above the kneecap. Important: Shawls wrapped over tank tops are rejected at the Bakan Tower upper sanctuary entrance.

RULE 02 SACRED GROUND

Sacred Relics & Monks

Never touch bas-relief carvings or sit on fallen pediments. Always ask politely before photographing monks. Women must never touch a Buddhist monk nor hand items directly into their hands.

RULE 03 SANCTUARY PEACE

Quiet Zones & Smoke-Free

Maintain serene volume around prayer shrines and stupas. The entire 401 km² archaeological reserve is strictly smoke-free. Littering incurs immediate $25 on-the-spot environmental penalties.

RULE 04 CHILD PROTECTION

Begging & Souvenir Children

Never buy souvenirs or give cash/candy to children at temple portals. Doing so encourages school truancy and exploitative networks. Support registered vocational schools (e.g., Artisans d'Angkor) instead.

payments Crisp USD Notes Rule

U.S. Dollars circulate freely alongside Cambodian Riel. However, Cambodian vendors and banks will strictly reject any USD note with tiny tears, pencil marks, pinholes, or heavy fold degradation.

Micro change (< $1): Always returned in Riel (~4,000 KHR)
favorite Tipping & Gratuity Norms

Tipping is not legally mandated, yet profoundly appreciated given local hospitality wage structures.

  • Tuk-Tuk Drivers (Short hops): 2,000 – 4,000 KHR (~$0.50 – $1)

  • Full-Day Licensed Temple Guides: $15 – $25 USD / day

The Art of the Sompeah (សំពះ)

5 LEVELS OF REVERENCE

The height of hands matches social status: chest-level for peers, mouth-level for elders/bosses, nose-level for parents and grandparents, eyebrow-level for monks & royalty, and forehead-level for Buddha and sacred deities.

Behavioral Decorum

Mindful Immersion in Khmer Society

Khmer hospitality is world-renowned for gentle warmth. Respecting these subtle cultural boundaries instantly unlocks deep local goodwill.

psychology

The Sacred Head & Modest Feet

In Cambodian cosmology, the head is spiritually the purest apex of the body—never touch anyone’s head, not even affectionately patting children. Conversely, feet are considered spiritually unclean: never point your soles toward monks, elders, or Buddha icons. Tuck your legs under you when seated on pagoda mats.

footprint

Slip Off Footwear Indoors

Always take off shoes and hats before crossing into living rooms of private homes, viharas (temple sanctums), and many local boutiques or boutique spas. Look for shoe racks or grouped sandals by the threshold.

mood

The Culture of "Saving Face" & Calm Tones

Public displays of anger, shouting, or aggressive confrontation cause acute loss of face in Khmer culture and will immediately shut down cooperation. Address delays or misunderstandings with a light smile, calm posture, and polite deference.

Climate & Gear

Seasonal Intelligence & Packing Essentials

Cambodia has three distinct climate windows. Match your travel window to avoid midday temple heat exhaustion or wet-season track conditions.

COOL & DRY 24°C – 31°C

November to February

Peak expedition season with crisp clear skies, gentle morning breezes, and minimal humidity. Perfect for trekking the Cardamom Mountains and all-day temple exploration.

Expedition Tip: Book boutique hotels & guides 2–3 months ahead.
HOT & RADIANT 33°C – 39°C

March to May

Intense equatorial sun warms sandstone ruins by 11 AM. The traditional Khmer New Year (Choul Chnam Thmey) bursts across mid-April with festive water celebrations.

Expedition Tip: Tour from 5:30 AM – 10:30 AM, rest mid-day, resume at 3:30 PM.
GREEN & MONSOON 27°C – 32°C

June to October

The landscape turns vibrant emerald green with full temple moats and dramatic afternoon skies. Rain usually occurs in short, spectacular 45-minute late-day cloudbursts.

Expedition Tip: Zero tourist crowds; superb photography and low prices.
